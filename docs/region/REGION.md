Status: draft for the director (Task 13, step 1), 2026-10-19. Nothing here is approved yet.

## The shape in one paragraph

The region is shaped like Australia and is called the **Crux region** for now (after the stars
of the Southern Cross; naming is cheap, D-012). You start on the cool south-east coast, head up
to the big harbour city, cross the snow country, follow the river inland into the dry country,
go deep into the red centre, out to the west coast, along the tropical north, and down into the
oldest rainforest. A train brings you home, and the Pokémon League waits on the wild island you
could see from your bedroom window on day one. The journey is one big loop, so the ending is
across the water from where it began.

Real places inspire the towns; no town uses a real town's name. No town, gym or story place
sits on a real sacred site or borrows an Aboriginal place name (see the note at the end).

## Biomes

| Biome | Where | What it feels like |
|-------|-------|--------------------|
| Temperate forest and coast | South-east, south-west corner, the island | Tall gum forest, ferny gullies, heath, surf beaches, cold southern sea |
| Cities | Port Halyard (east), Westhaven (west) | A harbour city of ferries and bridges; a quieter river city on the far coast |
| Alpine | Snowgum Heights, the island's summit | Snow gums, high plains, ski huts, mossy bogs |
| Outback | The inland: mallee, red centre, the far north-west | Saltbush plains, red sand, mesas, mining towns, enormous sky |
| Tropical north and reef | The top of the map | Mangroves, floodplains, crocodile rivers, the reef offshore |
| Rainforest | The north-east coast | Ancient rainforest down to the sea, waterfalls, mist |

## The order the player meets them

Wild levels and trainers rise with each leg; the regional dex placement comes later (spec §6.4).

1. **Lyrebird Cove** (home town, temperate coast). A fishing and surf town at the end of the
   road. Professor Acacia's research station sits on the headland by the lighthouse.
2. **Route 1** (coastal heath). Banksia scrub and dunes behind the beach; the first catches.
3. **Route 2** (tall gum forest). A forest track that crosses a creek at the ford and climbs
   toward the hills; the first route trainers and the first sighting of the villain team.
4. **Wattlebrook, Gym 1: Flying.** A timber town under huge old gum trees, loud with birds.
   It is spring, swooping season. *End of the first-gym slice.*
5. **Route 3** (hinterland farms to the coast road). Dairy country down to the sea.
6. **Port Halyard, Gym 2: Water.** The harbour city: ferries, a great arched bridge, beaches,
   the region's biggest Pokémon Centre and department store.
7. **Routes 4 and 5** (escarpment, then the high plains). The road leaves the coast, climbs a
   sandstone escarpment and rises into the snow country.
8. **Snowgum Heights, Gym 3: Ice.** A ski village above the tree line with stone huts and a
   chairlift; wild herds roam the high plains around it.
9. **Route 6** (river gorge). Down off the mountains along the young river.
10. **Redgum Wharf** (river town). Paddle-steamers, river red gums, a lock and weir; no gym.
11. **Route 7** (irrigation country into the mallee). Orchards give way to dry scrub.
12. **Mallee Downs, Gym 4: Fire.** A wheat and wool town on the edge of the dry country,
    with a grain silo painted with a huge mural and a volunteer fire brigade at its heart.
13. **Route 8** (saltbush and salt lakes). Flat, white and shimmering.
14. **Opal Hollow, Gym 5: Rock.** An opal-mining town built underground to escape the heat:
    dugout homes, a dugout church, fossils turned to opal in the seams.
15. **Route 9** (gibber plain, mesas). Stone desert and lonely tracks.
16. **Telegraph Wells** (outback crossroads). An old telegraph station by a spring; the
    roadhouse in the middle of everything; no gym.
17. **Route 10** (the long desert crossing). The hardest overland stretch, with water stops.
18. **Westhaven** (west coast city). A calm river city with a port; the villain team's
    laboratory hides in its industrial docks; no gym.
19. **Route 11** (wildflower country north up the coast). Spring wildflowers, then red ranges.
20. **Ironvale, Gym 6: Steel.** An iron-ore town in red gorge country: haul trucks, ore trains
    two kilometres long, a port loading ships.
21. **Route 12** (gorge country into the tropics). Boab trees, red cliffs, then the wet season.
22. **Mangrove Landing, Gym 7: Poison.** A stilt town on a tidal river in the floodplain
    country, where the rangers fight introduced toads and the jellyfish season shuts the beaches.
23. **Route 13** (the gulf savanna). Termite mounds, wide rivers, crocodile crossings.
24. **Coralhaven** (reef town). The gateway to the reef; diving, a turtle hospital; no gym.
25. **Route 14** (into the rainforest). The road climbs into cloud and the trees close in.
26. **Mistwood, Gym 8: Dragon.** A town in the oldest rainforest in the world, where the
    forest runs down to the reef. The story's climax happens in the old growth beyond it.
27. **Coast Line** (train). After the climax the train carries you home down the east coast.
28. **Ferry and Victory Road.** From Lyrebird Cove the ferry crosses the strait to the island,
    where Victory Road climbs through wet forest and snow.
29. **Crux Summit, the Pokémon League.** Four Elite trainers and the Champion on the
    island's peak.

Later areas (post-league, the reef underwater, side islands) are left out on purpose.

## The eight gym towns and the league

- **Gym 1, Wattlebrook (Flying).** Gym puzzle: rope walkways through the canopy; gusts from
  open shutters push you along. Trainers are birdwatchers and arborists.
- **Gym 2, Port Halyard (Water).** In the surf club on the harbour beach: a pool with lanes,
  floating pontoons that shift as you step on switches. Trainers are lifesavers and swimmers.
- **Gym 3, Snowgum Heights (Ice).** An ice rink and ski slope: slide puzzles on ice.
- **Gym 4, Mallee Downs (Fire).** The fire station and training yard: drills with hoses and
  doors that only open after a drill is done.
- **Gym 5, Opal Hollow (Rock).** Down a working mine shaft: lanterns, mine carts, tunnels.
- **Gym 6, Ironvale (Steel).** The ore crusher and conveyor yard: moving belts to ride.
- **Gym 7, Mangrove Landing (Poison).** Boardwalks over the mangroves; the tide changes
  which walks are open.
- **Gym 8, Mistwood (Dragon).** Inside a ring of ancient trees; mist hides the path and the
  trainers' positions.
- **Pokémon League, Crux Summit.** Elite Four (Fighting, Ghost, Fairy, Dark) and a Champion
  with a mixed team and a Mega Evolution. They are named in the gym leaders task (spec §6.3).

## Gym table (for approval)

The level cap is the level your Pokémon cannot pass until that gym is beaten (D-022, hard
mode only). Each leader's ace sits at the cap, as in Radical Red, so on hard you meet the ace
at the same level as your best Pokémon, never above it.

| Town | Type | Leader | Level cap | Ace |
|------|------|--------|-----------|-----|
| Wattlebrook | Flying | Wren, an arborist who knows every nest in town | **14** | Staravia |
| Port Halyard | Water | Marlo, captain of the harbour surf club | 21 | Azumarill |
| Snowgum Heights | Ice | Inge, head of the ski patrol | 28 | Sneasel |
| Mallee Downs | Fire | Kel, captain of the volunteer fire brigade | 35 | Camerupt |
| Opal Hollow | Rock | Hettie, an old opal miner and fossil hunter | 42 | Aerodactyl |
| Ironvale | Steel | Dane, an engineer who runs the ore trains | 49 | Metagross |
| Mangrove Landing | Poison | Tully, a wetlands ranger | 56 | Tentacruel |
| Mistwood | Dragon | Dr Elsie Rowe, a palaeontologist | 63 | Garchomp |
| Crux Summit | League | Elite Four, then the Champion | 72 | Champion's ace at 72 |

**Gym 1 cap: 14.** Radical Red's first cap is 15 after a longer opening that includes a whole
forest. Our slice is home town, two routes, then the gym, so one level
lower keeps the same feel: you arrive around 11 to 13 if you battle every trainer, and the last
levels come from the gym's own trainers. Starters begin at 5.

**How the caps rise.** Seven levels per gym from 14 to 63, then nine to the league, because
Victory Road and the Elite Four are a longer, harder stretch. Steady steps make every gap the
same length of play to plan around. The aces follow the evolution levels: Staravia evolves at
14, Azumarill at 18, Tentacruel at 30, Camerupt at 33, Metagross at 45, Garchomp at 48, so each ace is fully
evolved at its cap or is a sensible earlier stage.

**Starter coverage of the early gyms** (starters are in STORY.md). Against Flying, Treecko is
at a disadvantage and Cyndaquil and Totodile are neutral; against Water, Treecko is strong and
Cyndaquil weak; against Ice, Cyndaquil is strong and Treecko weak. No starter breezes through,
and no starter is stuck for long.

> **Director to choose:** the first gym's type.
> 1. **Flying (recommended).** Swooping season is a warm, true Australian spring moment, and no
>    starter has a big edge. Hard mode gets a tough Intimidate ace in Staravia.
> 2. **Bug.** Cicadas and beetles in the gum forest; classic early-game feel, but Cyndaquil
>    wins it easily and Treecko struggles.

## Notes and limits

- **Map toolchain first (D-020).** These places become maps only after the text map source,
  converter and preview renderer exist. Route lengths here are a feel, not a tile count.
- **First Nations culture.** Australia's First Nations peoples and places are real and living.
  This draft avoids sacred sites, Aboriginal place names and invented "Dreaming" lore, because
  doing those well needs real consultation, not invention. Leaving them out entirely is itself
  a choice worth the director's thought before the story grows past the slice.
- **What waits for later tasks.** Gym teams and the Elite Four (spec §6.3), the regional dex
  and where each Pokémon lives (§6.4), and the ten regional forms (§6.5).
- **On approval** the caps go into `content/progression.json` (Task 13, step 2).
